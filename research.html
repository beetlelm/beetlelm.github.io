Research Projects

New ways to study how language models learn

Beetle opens up new opportunities to study how language models acquire, organise, and use linguistic knowledge.

By varying the languages models encounter, the order in which they encounter them, and the amount of data they learn from, we can investigate how different learning experiences shape model behaviour.

The framework supports research across linguistics, cognitive science, machine learning, and interpretability. Researchers can use Beetle to explore existing questions, test new hypotheses, and develop methods for understanding learning in language models.

Who it's for

Audiences we're building Beetle for

Beetle is an open resource: you don't need to build a giant AI to explore the science of how language models learn. It is useful whether you train models yourself or simply want to understand how AI acquires language.

AI researchers, multilingual NLP & model builders

Use the open models and resources as a starting point for comparing training strategies and testing hypotheses — or train your own models and ask your own questions.

What you can do with Beetle
  • Study learning dynamics. Analyse how capabilities develop over training, in the spirit of shared tasks like Parameter Golf, BabyLM and the NanoGPT Speedrun.
  • Analyse data mixtures. What happens if a model learns two languages from the start, learns them sequentially, or encounters its second language much later?
  • Developmental interpretability. Use the Beetle checkpoints to study how models learn and how capabilities form during training.
  • Multilinguality. Beetle covers 21 first languages, including script-independent and typologically diverse settings such as Finnish, Basque, Hindi, Japanese, Korean, Turkish and Chinese — a testbed for cross-lingual transfer.
  • Data scale and quality. Vary language-exposure curricula and training scales (100M, 2B and 24B tokens) to investigate which training conditions drive observed differences.
  • Infrastructure. Reuse the Beetle tooling for tokenisation and memorisation experiments.

Cognitive scientists & the simply curious

You don't need to build a giant AI to explore the science. Beetle is a way in for cognitive scientists, applied researchers, and anyone curious about how AI learns language.

What you can do with Beetle
  • Non-technical exploration. Explore how AI learns languages without training anything yourself — load a model in the browser and watch it in action.
  • Cognitive modelling. Use Beetle to study how well model predictions align with human cognitive data, from reading times to grammaticality judgements.
  • Connect computation and cognition. Compare model learning trajectories with patterns observed in human language acquisition and bilingual processing.

Research directions

Questions Beetle makes tractable

Language learning and human cognition

How do models' learning trajectories compare with patterns observed in human language acquisition and processing? Beetle provides a setting for investigating relationships between computational learning and human linguistic behaviour.

Multilingual learning and cross-lingual transfer

How does experience with one language shape a model's ability to learn or use another? Explore how language background, exposure, and learning order influence cross-lingual generalisation.

The development of linguistic representations

How do internal representations emerge and change during training? Beetle's checkpoints make it possible to investigate how models develop linguistic knowledge, and how these changes relate to their behaviour.

Continual learning and interference

When does learning something new reinforce, compete with, or disrupt existing knowledge? Beetle offers a setting for studying how models retain and adapt linguistic knowledge as their experience changes.

Tokenisation and the structure of language

How does the way text is represented influence what models learn? Researchers can investigate the relationship between tokenisation, linguistic structure, learning efficiency, and generalisation.

Learning dynamics, data, and scale

Which aspects of a model's learning experience matter most? Compare models trained with different amounts of data and different learning conditions to investigate how training choices shape the emergence of linguistic capabilities.

Start with a question

From a question to a controlled experiment

Beetle is designed to make research questions about language learning experimentally tractable. You can begin with an existing checkpoint, compare models with different training histories, or use the framework to develop a new controlled experiment.

We welcome collaborations with researchers interested in language acquisition, multilingualism, representation learning, interpretability, and the broader science of language models.
