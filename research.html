Research

What can language models teach us about learning a second language?

Beetle is an open research resource for studying bilingualism, second-language learning, and how language models acquire and represent language.

By controlling what models learn, which languages they encounter, and when they encounter them, Beetle makes it possible to investigate questions about human language processing and machine learning that are difficult to study with existing models.

Whether you work in psycholinguistics, computational linguistics, interpretability, or multilingual machine learning, you can use Beetle's models to test hypotheses, reproduce findings, and develop new research directions.

Research directions

One model family, many research questions

Human language processing

Can language models help explain how people learn and process a second language?

Use Beetle to investigate how models predict human reading times, distinguish grammatical from ungrammatical sentences, and respond to linguistic context. Compare model behaviour with experimental findings from bilingual and second-language research.

You do not need to train a language model from scratch to get started. Released checkpoints let you explore existing results, test alternative hypotheses, and connect computational predictions to human behaviour.

Possible research directions
  • Compare model predictions with reading-time and grammaticality-judgement data.
  • Test whether models reproduce differences between first-language and second-language processing.
  • Investigate which properties of human bilingualism are captured by language models, and which are not.
  • Develop computational tests of theories of second-language acquisition.

Cross-lingual transfer and representation learning

How does knowledge learned in one language influence another?

Beetle's controlled training setups let you investigate how language relationships, exposure schedules, and training scale affect what models learn. Study when knowledge transfers across languages, which language pairs benefit most, and how these effects change during training.

Possible research directions
  • Compare cross-lingual transfer across languages with different linguistic properties.
  • Trace how shared and language-specific representations develop during training.
  • Test whether exposure order changes which capabilities emerge and when.
  • Investigate whether improvements in one language come at the expense of another.

Mechanistic interpretability and model analysis

What changes inside a model as it learns multiple languages?

Beetle provides model families and training checkpoints that can be used to study the development of linguistic representations and the mechanisms underlying cross-lingual behaviour. Its controlled conditions make it possible to compare models that differ in a particular aspect of their training experience.

Possible research directions
  • Track the emergence of linguistic features across training checkpoints.
  • Compare internal representations across languages, curricula, and model scales.
  • Investigate whether the mechanisms supporting a capability are shared across languages.
  • Connect changes in model representations to measurable changes in behaviour.

New projects and student collaborations

Have a question that Beetle could help answer?

Beetle is designed to support research beyond the experiments in our paper. We welcome collaborations with students and researchers interested in developing new projects using the released models, checkpoints, evaluation tasks, and training framework.

Projects can range from analysing existing checkpoints to designing new experiments, developing evaluation methods, or training controlled model variants. The aim is to turn promising questions into rigorous, reproducible research with a clear path towards publication.

You do not need to have a fully developed project before getting in touch. If you have an interesting question about language learning, multilingual models, or interpretability, we would be happy to explore it with you.

Why Beetle

A controlled setting for studying language learning

Existing multilingual models often differ in several ways at once, making it difficult to determine why they behave differently. Beetle addresses this problem by allowing researchers to vary four aspects of training independently.

Language exposure

Compare balanced bilingual training with curricula that introduce languages at different times or in different proportions.

First language

Investigate how a model's initial language experience affects subsequent learning across 21 first-language settings.

Training scale

Compare models trained on 100 million, 2 billion, and 24 billion tokens to study how learning changes with additional experience.

Tokenisation

Use a common BPE tokeniser with equal-compression controls to help isolate the effects of training conditions from differences in tokenisation.

The result is a family of models that supports controlled comparisons, rather than a collection of unrelated multilingual systems.   Explore the framework · Browse models and data

Key findings

What we have found so far

Our first study uses Beetle to investigate how bilingual training experience shapes second-language behaviour.

Training order matters

Staged curricula improve alignment with human second-language reading times compared with balanced bilingual exposure.

The effects depend on scale

Differences between training curricula are particularly pronounced at smaller training budgets.

Language relationships matter

The benefits of structured exposure are largest for typologically closer language pairs in our experiments.

Human processing measures tell different stories

The curricula that best predict reading times are not necessarily those that perform best on grammaticality judgements, suggesting that online processing and offline linguistic judgements capture different aspects of model behaviour.

These findings are a starting point, not a limit on what the models can be used to study.

EMNLP 2026 (Oral, top 3%)

Beetle: A Bilingual Model Suite for Modelling Second-Language Processing

Suchir Salhan1 Catherine Arnett2 James A. Michaelov3 Paula Buttery1

1University of Cambridge  ·  2EleutherAI  ·  3University of Oxford  ·  Correspondence: sas245@cam.ac.uk

Overview Beetle introduces a controlled pretraining framework and releases 285 bilingual and 45 monolingual open-source language models, with checkpoints spanning multiple first languages, training scales, and exposure schedules. Evaluating against human bilingual and second-language reading-time prediction and grammaticality-judgement tasks, we find that staged and temporally structured curricula consistently improve alignment with language-learner reading time compared with balanced bilingual training — with the largest gains at smaller data scales and for typologically closer language pairs.
