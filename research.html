Research

Don't just study what AI can do. Study how it learns.

🪲 Beetle is an open-source playground for experimenting with how language models learn.

What happens when a model learns two languages instead of one? When it encounters a new language later in training? When it learns from more data?

With 330 models, 21 first languages, five language-learning setups and thousands of training checkpoints, Beetle lets you investigate these questions for yourself.

For everyone

What can you do with Beetle?

🔬 AI enthusiasts, researchers & model builders

Get hands-on with how AI learns.

  • 🧠 Study how capabilities develop. Track what changes as models learn.
  • 🌍 Experiment with multilingual AI. Compare how models learn languages together, separately or in sequence.
  • 🔍 Look inside models. Investigate representations, memorisation and interpretability.
  • ⚙️ Build your own models. Change training data, language mixtures and learning strategies.
  • 📈 Explore different scales. Compare models trained on 100M, 2B and 24B tokens.

Start with an existing model or train your own.

🧠 Cognitive scientists, linguists & curious minds

Explore what AI can teach us about learning.

  • 👶 Study language acquisition. Compare how AI learns with how humans acquire language.
  • 🗣️ Investigate bilingualism. Explore how learning one language shapes another.
  • 📖 Connect AI and cognition. Compare model predictions with human reading times and grammaticality judgements.
  • 🔬 Discover the science of AI. Explore models and their learning without building one yourself.

No machine-learning expertise required to get started.

Open questions

What can we discover?

Beetle makes it possible to investigate questions that finished models alone cannot answer.

  • Multilingual learning: How do languages influence one another?
  • Emerging capabilities: When and how do models acquire new abilities?
  • Human cognition: Where do AI learning patterns resemble human language acquisition?
  • Continual learning: Does new knowledge strengthen or interfere with existing knowledge?
  • Tokenisation: How does the way text is represented shape learning?
  • Data and scale: Which training conditions make the biggest difference?

These are starting points, not limits. Ask your own questions.
